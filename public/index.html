BEHIND THE FORECAST

A transparent way to read a match.

1. Choose your competition and dates

Browse every match returned for the selected date range, with search, status filters and pagination. “All leagues” lists the matches your connected provider can return for one UTC date. API plans may restrict competitions, seasons, forecasts and update speed.

2. Understand the source

API-Football supplies primary three-way forecasts. When those are unavailable, football-data.org can supply match history for JK’s experimental goal model. Fallback is possible only where the league and the same match can be verified. We never silently substitute a different game.

3. Read the probabilities

Home, draw and away total 100%. The predicted outcome is the largest probability; a draw can be the leading outcome. A favorite can still be more likely to draw or lose than win. A close forecast may produce a recommendation to skip the match.

4. Treat tips as probability-based suggestions

Double chance combines two mutually exclusive match outcomes. Goals and both-teams-to-score suggestions appear only when the goal model supplies estimates. When the market consensus is the source, probabilities come from bookmaker prices with the margin removed, so they carry no edge over the bookmakers; otherwise no bookmaker prices are used. Either way we cannot claim profitable odds or value. All markets refer to 90 minutes plus stoppage time.

Expected goals and line-ups

Where StrikerAPI covers the competition, the analysis adds expected goals for and against per match for both clubs, with the gap to the goals actually scored, and, for matches you picked, the confirmed starting elevens once they are named about an hour before kickoff. Forecasts made earlier did not know the line-ups.

Closing line value

Picks made while the bookmakers’ consensus is available remember it, and the app records the consensus again just before kickoff. The difference is the closing line value: over many picks, a market that keeps moving your way is the one sign of judgement that does not depend on results. A handful of picks proves nothing either way.

What “accurate” means here

No forecast makes results predictable: the bookmakers’ consensus, the best predictor there is, sees its favourite lose about 45% of the time. So the forecast shown is the best available number (the consensus, refreshed close to kickoff when the line moves; the models only where there is no line), and the “most likely in this match” ladder ranks every selection by chance so the sure things and the coin flips are told apart. The live forecast record below measures how the shown chances really perform.

Scores and handicaps

The correct-score grid and the Asian handicap lines come from one scoreline matrix. For a forecast led by the bookmakers’ consensus, that matrix is fitted to their own line and total, so every market on the page tells the same story; for a model forecast it is the model’s matrix. Whole-goal handicaps can push, and their fair odds leave the push out.

The betting slip view

The same matrix prices every market a Swiss betting slip lists, in its order and wording: final result, double chance, both teams to score, over/under, the three-way handicap, halftime/fulltime, the first-half markets, the first goal and the correct score. Each cell shows the chance and the price to insist on; hold the slip next to it and compare by eye. Selections from the full-time markets can be priced together as a same-match combination, counted over the scorelines where every leg holds rather than multiplied. First-half markets assume 45% of the goals come before the break.

Prices, value and stakes

Every chance is also shown as fair decimal odds (1 ÷ chance, no margin). The value check compares a bookmaker’s price with that: a price above the fair odds has positive expected value by the forecast, a price below it has none, and the suggested stake is a quarter of the Kelly optimum capped at 2% of a virtual bankroll kept in your browser. When the forecast is the bookmakers’ consensus, a better price elsewhere is a real difference between bookmakers; when it is the app’s model, small edges are noise. The slip calculator multiplies the chances of the picks you tick, which treats them as independent: every extra leg multiplies the chance of losing the lot. These are sums, not advice.

Experimental model limitations

The fallback rates every team on attack and defence using recent regulation-time results from football-data.org, including each team’s other competitions. Newer matches count more, ratings are shrunk toward the average, and the opponent’s strength is taken into account. Home, draw and away chances come from independent Poisson goal distributions. It was tested on about 1,800 league matches from 2025/26 across the five major leagues (each predicted only from earlier results): it clearly beats naive baselines and the older model, but is a little less accurate than bookmaker closing prices. It has not been tested on Champions League fixtures, ignores injuries, lineups and rotation, and can compare teams from different leagues only roughly. When a team has too little history, an older, simpler goal-average model is used, and when even that is not possible there is no forecast.

Pick records

Each pick preserves its original forecast. You can remove a pick until kickoff; after kickoff, or once it has a result, it is locked and cannot be changed or removed, so results cannot be hidden. Results are checked on request, three eligible matches at a time. Delayed, postponed or incomplete results stay pending. Extra-time and penalty outcomes are not substituted for regulation-time scores. Browser profiles are separate from the earlier ChatGPT-hosted app’s accounts and picks.
